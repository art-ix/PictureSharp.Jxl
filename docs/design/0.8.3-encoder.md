# Encoder work for 0.8.3

Status: released in 0.8.3. Base: `647e367` (`v0.8.2`).
Measurements: [speed.md](../benchmarks/speed.md#encoder-work-083) and
[encoder-0.8.3.json](../measurements/encoder-0.8.3.json).

0.8.3 is the first release under the user decisions of 2026-10-29: the encoder may change its bytes when file size
stays within ±0.5% and ssimulacra2/butteraugli do not get worse on the test set (decision 2), and lossless efforts
follow libjxl's Modular tiers with effort 7 unchanged (decision 5). The decoder is not changed.

## Method

Profiles (dotnet-trace, CPU 0, `DOTNET_TieredCompilation=0`, one thread) of v0.8.2 lossy e3/e7 and lossless e1/e7
chose the candidates. Each candidate was built as a separate snapshot and timed against its predecessor with
alternating A/B, B/A processes (4 rounds × 3 warm calls); it was kept only with at least 2% on its target and no
control above +1%. The final matrix compares v0.8.2 with the final source (`scripts/measure-paired.py`).

| Profile (v0.8.2, 1 thread) | Hottest frames |
| --- | --- |
| lossy photo d1 e7 | `JxlAcStrategySearch.SumChannelsAvx2` 17.4% exclusive (EstimateEntropy 56.9% inclusive), CfL `DerivativesAvx2` 6.3% |
| lossy photo d1 e3 | `JxlXybInput.ConvertAvx2` 16.6%, `JxlEntropyEncoder.WriteStream` 7.2%, `JxlAcTokenizer.Tokenize` 5.5% |
| lossless photo d0 e7 | `JxlTreeLearner.Search.Evaluate` 25.6%, `TokenizeRecorded` 17.1%, `Scan` 10.3%, `RctCost` 6.7% |
| lossless photo d0 e1 (first tier build) | `WriteStream` 33.4%, `TokenizeGradient` 22.7%, token/value histograms 12% |
| lossless screen d0 e1 (first tier build) | palette `Dictionary`/`HashSet` 60% |

## Lossy: lane-ordered sums (output may change)

Until 0.8.2 the AVX2 kernels of `EstimateEntropy` (AC strategy search) and the CfL Newton derivatives computed their
terms eight at a time but added them in scalar order, to keep 0.7.10 bit identity. These serial float/double
additions were most of the remaining exclusive time. Both now accumulate eight lanes (element `i` in lane `i % 8`)
and reduce them in one fixed order: lane `k` plus lane `k + 4`, then the even pair plus the odd pair. This is how
libjxl's Highway code accumulates these sums. The scalar fallback uses the same lanes and order, so scalar, SSE4.1
and AVX2 builds still produce identical bytes; a coefficient left out of the CfL sums adds +0 to its lane in both.

The rounding of the sums changes, so a strategy or CfL decision whose costs are within rounding of each other can
flip. Gate (decision 2), v0.8.2 against 0.8.3 encoders on 12 images (8/16-bit photo, screen, crops with alpha and
gray, the 12 MP photo at d1 e7) × distances 0.5/1/2/3/6 × efforts 1/3/5/7, plus the golden's re-encoded inputs:
241 + 6 encodes, of which 6 differ. All 6 have exactly the same size; their djxl decodes are pixel-identical to the
v0.8.2 decodes (ssimulacra2 and butteraugli identical), with 3 to 140 differing bytes. The gate holds trivially.

| Candidate | Target | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EstimateEntropy lane sums | lossy e7 | photo −4.9%, screen −5.5%, e5 −6.0% (kept) |
| CfL derivative lane sums | lossy e7 | photo −3.8%, screen −3.6% (kept; e3 does not use them) |

## Lossless effort tiers (output changes for efforts 1–6)

`JxlModularEffort` maps the effort to libjxl 0.12.0's `ModularFrameEncoder` settings (see
[encoding](../encoding.md#lossless-frames) for the table):

- efforts 1–4 code RGB as YCoCg (`rct_type` 6) without the RCT search, as libjxl does above `kHare`;
- effort 2 uses `PredefinedTree(kGradientFixedDC)` and effort 3 `PredefinedTree(kWPFixedDC)`, ported from
  `MakeFixedTree` (33 cutoffs, a minimum gap of 8 × (14 − ⌈log2 pixels⌉) cutoffs below 2^14 pixels, cutoffs × 2^min(4, bits − 11)
  above 11 bits); like libjxl's, they have no channel splits, so all channels share their contexts;
- efforts 4–6 learn with the first 3/4/5 splitting properties, `max_property_values` 32/48/64, sample fraction
  0.5 × `nb_repeats` 0.3/0.5/0.7 and node threshold 159/145/131;
- effort 7 is the 0.8.2 encoder (7 properties, 96 values, 50%, threshold 117); efforts 8 and 9 behave as 7.

libjxl's effort 1 is a separate encoder (`enc_fast_lossless`: prefix codes, run lengths, its own bitstream layout).
Porting it is out of scope; effort 1 here keeps the ANS bitstream with one gradient leaf per channel. It is smaller
than `cjxl -e 1` and several times slower; efforts 2–7 are within a few percent of `cjxl` at the same effort.
The palette (≤ 1024 colours) is tried at every effort, channel compaction (see below) for > 8-bit input at every
effort; libjxl's per-group palettes and 8-bit channel compaction (efforts 2+) are not implemented.

Trees whose decisions are all on the gradient property and whose leaves all predict the gradient (efforts 1–2, and
any learned single gradient leaf) are tokenized by `TokenizeGradient` without running the weighted predictor: the
first row and column use `JxlNeighbours`' edge substitutions, interior rows read W, N and NW directly, and the tokens
are appended row by row (`JxlTokens.Append`). The tokens are those `TokenVisitor` produces.

## Lossless speed without output change

| Change | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `JxlEntropyEncoder.WriteStream`: the backward ANS pass records each symbol's renormalization chunk and raw bits as one packed word; `JxlBitWriter.WritePacked` writes them with one capacity check per batch | WriteStream 36% → 29% inclusive at e1 |
| `TokenHistograms`: one flat count table (default-config tokens of 32-bit values stay below 128), trimmed to the last used token | removes per-token array growth checks |
| Palette discovery and index mapping: an open-addressing table of at most 1025 keys at ≤ 25% load instead of `HashSet<long>`/`Dictionary<long,int>` | screen e1 106 → 41 ms |

All three produce identical bytes (checked on every tier and in the golden set). The first full measurement found
two allocation regressions in them, fixed before release: `WritePacked` reserved the worst case (7 bytes per symbol),
which grew every retained group section (12 MP lossless: +284 MiB allocated, +590 MiB peak RSS); it now sums the
entries' bit counts and reserves exactly. The flat count table was sized contexts × 128 and added about 7 MiB to every
lossy encode (thousands of AC contexts); it now allocates a row per context that occurs. The colour sets are merged and
sorted exactly as before, so the palette does not depend on table order.

## Rejected

| Candidate | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Tree learner: both predictors' bin histograms in one pass over the samples | lossless photo e7 −0.3%, 16-bit +0.0% |

Not attempted: a cheaper cube root in `JxlXybInput` (the lossy e3 hotspot) would change every lossy file and needs
its own quality gate; unchecked indexing (`Unsafe.Add`, decision 1) in the learner's histogram loop would need
per-sample validation of bin and token values, so no measured gain justified it and `SkipLocalsInit` stays off.

## 16-bit lossless: palette and channel compaction (effort 7 changes for > 8-bit input only)

User decision (2026-10-29, after the first measurements): lossless effort-7 output may change for input of more than
8 bits to close two gaps against `cjxl`; 8-bit output stays byte-identical at every effort 7–9, and the efforts 1–6
tiers get the same fixes.

- **Palette above 32767.** Palette keys pack 16 bits per sample and decoded them as signed, so 16-bit samples above
  32767 rejected the palette: a widened 8-bit screen (≤ 1024 colours) was coded with the learned tree. Each row band
  now records whether it saw negative samples or samples above 32767; a frame with both still has no palette, otherwise
  the keys are read as signed or unsigned accordingly. 8-bit and signed inputs decode their keys as before.
- **Channel compaction** (libjxl's single-channel palette, `channel_colors_pre_transform_percent`, enabled by libjxl
  for > 8-bit input at every effort): for input of more than 8 bits, each channel whose occurring values are few
  becomes a sorted palette (a meta channel) and indices, before the colour transform. Conditions: at most one value per
  16 pixels (libjxl), at most a quarter of the value range (libjxl: 95%), and a lower gradient-residual cost of sampled
  rows than the original channel (as libjxl's slower efforts check every transform with `EstimateCost`). The tighter
  conditions came from measurements: at 95% a full-range 16-bit photo (51–78% of its range used) grew by 0.95%, and
  without the cost check a synthetic channel whose values wrap at 16 bits grew by 118%.
- After compaction of a colour channel, efforts without the RCT search (1–4) choose between no transform and YCoCg
  with the RCT cost estimate instead of always taking YCoCg: indices of different scales can code worse in YCoCg.

Effort-7 sizes, v0.8.2 → 0.8.3 (`cjxl -d 0 -e 7` 0.12.0 in the last column), all decoded exactly by djxl:

## 16-bit encoding time (decision 6)

Target: 16-bit encode time ≤ 1.1× the 8-bit time of the same image. `photo1w-16` (the 8-bit 1 MP photo widened
× 257, new in `make-speed-inputs.py`) has exactly the 8-bit content; `screen-16` is the widened screen. The other
16-bit photos are djxl renders with real 16-bit noise and are not the same image. Ratios are in
[speed.md](../benchmarks/speed.md#encoder-work-083). Before the palette fix lossless `screen-16` took 1.54× the 8-bit
time (no palette, learned tree on 16-bit samples).
