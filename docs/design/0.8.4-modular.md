# Modular decoding work for 0.8.4

Status: released in 0.8.4. Base: `7f7a353` (0.8.3 plus README).
Measurements: [speed.md](../benchmarks/speed.md#modular-decoding-084) and
[modular-0.8.4.json](../measurements/modular-0.8.4.json).

0.8.4 targets lossless (Modular) decoding, where 0.8.3 took 1.9–2.5× djxl's single-thread time while VarDCT decoding
was within 1.2× of it. Output is unchanged: decoded pixels and encoded bytes are identical to 0.8.3 (golden SHA-256
88eccd4b… at 1 and 16 threads). The lossless encoder runs the same weighted predictor and gains from it too.

## Method

Profiles (dotnet-trace, CPU 0, one thread) of 0.8.3 decodes of `cjxl` files chose the candidates; JIT disassembly
(`DOTNET_JitDisasm`) and a stand-alone loop over the weighted predictor (3 channels of the 1 MP photo, no entropy
decoding) explained them. Candidates were timed against their predecessor with `artifacts/perf-0.8.4/quick.sh`;
the final matrix compares 0.8.3 with the final source (`scripts/measure-paired.py`), and the two small candidates were
measured separately against a build without them.

The trees `cjxl -e 7` writes decide only on properties 9–12 (gradient and neighbour differences) and 15 (weighted
predictor error), with leaves predicting the gradient or the weighted predictor; effort 3 decides on property 15 only,
effort 2 on the gradient only, and effort 1 has one leaf per channel and codes residuals with prefix codes and LZ77.
16-bit trees are 5–10 times larger (around 1000 nodes) than 8-bit ones.

| Profile (0.8.3, 1 thread) | Hottest frames |
| --- | --- |
| lossless photo d0 e7 | `JxlMaTree.DecodeChannel` 54% exclusive (weighted predictor inlined), tree `Lookup` 22%, `ReadClustered` 9% |
| lossless photo d0 e3 | `DecodeWithLookup` 74% exclusive (weighted predictor), `ReadClustered` 10% |
| lossless 16-bit photo d0 e7 (after the predictor change) | `Lookup` 37%, `DecodeChannel` 40%, `ReadClustered` 10% |
| lossless 12 MP photo d0 e1 | `ReadClusteredRow` 49% inclusive, `DecodeSingleLeaf` 21%, `WriteIntegerNarrowRow` 9.5% |
| lossless 12 MP photo d0 e2 | `DecodeWithLookup`/`GradientTreeRow` 68%, `ReadClustered` 28%, `WriteIntegerNarrowRow` 10% |

## Weighted predictor without branches

The weighted predictor alone (prediction and update, no entropy decoding) took 133 ms for the three channels of the
1 MP photo, more than djxl's whole effort-3 decode of it. RyuJIT emitted no conditional moves for it: the choice of
the largest error, the clamp to the neighbours' range, `Math.Max`/`Math.Min` and the overflow check of `Math.Abs` were
96 branches, and on photo data they are unpredictable. Clang compiles libjxl's version with conditional moves.

`JxlWeightedPredictor` now computes these with sign masks (`(a − b) >> 63`); the arguments are 32-bit errors and
samples with three extra bits, or the weighted average, which is a 64-bit product shifted right by 24 bits, so none of
the differences overflows. The four sub-predictors' errors are interleaved per position and read through
`MemoryMarshal`/`Unsafe.Add` (user decision 1) after one check of `x` against the row width; the header coefficients
are copied into fields. Stand-alone loop: 133 → 83 ms with the masks alone, 61 ms with the layout (identical values).
A frozen copy of the 0.8.3 predictor (`ScalarBaseline/JxlWeightedPredictorScalar.cs`) is compared value by value on
random headers, widths 1–17 and samples up to the 32-bit limits.

## Prefix-code rows

Effort-1 files code residuals with prefix codes and LZ77. Each symbol checked and refilled the bit buffer two or three
times through the `ref` bit reader, whose fields the JIT keeps in memory. `JxlBitReader.ReadRow` now keeps the
buffer in locals while 8 bytes remain in the current slice, refills once per symbol (a symbol and its raw bits are at
most 15 + 31 bits) and hands a generic `IJxlBitDecoder` the buffer; elsewhere every symbol takes the checked path, with
the same values, positions and failures. LZ77 literals use it up to the next length token. The decoder methods must be
inlined (`AggressiveInlining`): without it the buffer is address-exposed and the loop gains nothing.

## Integer pixel output

Lossless frames are written from integer samples: each sample was multiplied by 1 / (2^bits − 1) and dithered to
8 bits, or rounded to 16 bits, one pixel at a time. Each worker now scales a row per channel into a float row (vector
int-to-float conversion and multiply, the same rounding as the scalar conversion) and passes it to the vectorized
float row writers. A test compares the output with the frozen 0.7.10 writer for widths around the vector boundaries,
8–31 bits, gray, colour, alpha and both depths.

## Small candidates

| Candidate | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `PredictMixed`: leaves mixing the gradient and weighted predictors select between them with a mask instead of the 14-way `switch` (an indirect jump that mispredicts) | together: photo1 e7 −3.1%, photo12 e7 −3.0%, 16-bit −0.4% to −1.1% (kept) |
| Trees deciding on none of properties 2–8 compute only properties 9–14 per pixel (`JxlMaProperty.SetDifferences`) | measured with the row above |

## Rejected

| Candidate | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Interior pixels without edge substitutes (neighbours and weighted predictor) | e7 +2% (the edge branches are predictable) |
| `AggressiveInlining` on the predictor, tree lookup, `Predict` and `ReadClustered` | e7 +3%, e3 +17% |
| Branch-free tree descent (the right child follows the left one) | e7 +15%, 16-bit +7%: speculation down the predicted branch is faster |
| Tree descent without bounds checks | ±1% |
| ANS state in a local cursor for gradient-tree rows | e2 −0.6% |
| `AggressiveInlining` on `ClampedGradient` | e1 +34%, e2 +26% (register pressure in the row loops) |

## Results

1 thread, 0.8.3 → 0.8.4 (libjxl 1 thread in brackets): photo e3 185 → 99 ms (72), photo e7 277 → 181 (140), 16-bit
photo e7 365 → 266 (182), 12 MP e1 610 → 458 (335), e2 621 → 536 (411), e7 3351 → 2161 (1685). Lossless encoding
e3 −31%, e7 −10% (12 MP −19%). Full tables and controls: [speed.md](../benchmarks/speed.md#modular-decoding-084).

The 16-bit target (≤ 1.1× the 8-bit time) is not met by the decoder on photos with real 16-bit content (1.43–1.47×;
libjxl 1.28–1.30×): their trees are 5–10 times larger, and the tree descent, bound by branch mispredictions, is about
37% of the 16-bit decode. A widened 8-bit screen decodes in 0.98× the 8-bit time.
