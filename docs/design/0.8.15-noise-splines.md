# Photon noise and splines for 0.8.15

Status: implemented (2026-11-02), not committed; decisions 1a, 2a, 3a (user). Base: `304274e` (0.8.14).

0.8.15 is the sixth step of the feature series (roadmap in plan.md: 0.8.10 blending, 0.8.11 layers, 0.8.12 animation
decode, 0.8.13 frame reader, 0.8.14 animation encode, 0.8.15 noise and splines, 0.8.16 extra channels, 0.8.17
hardening, 0.9.0). Both features are frame-level image features of JPEG XL that are drawn by the decoder on top of
the decoded frame: noise is synthetic film grain from an 8-entry intensity LUT, splines are smooth strokes (for thin
lines and hair that DCT codes poorly).

Goal: `Image.Load`, `Image.Identify`, `Image.OpenFrameReaderAsync` and `JxlLayers` decode frames with noise or
splines as djxl 0.12.0 does (lossy within 1/255; noise is part of the decoded pixels, not random per run), with
identical results at 1 and N threads. The encoder gets `JxlEncoderOptions.PhotonNoiseIso` like
`cjxl --photon_noise_iso`. The encoder does not produce splines. Every existing result stays unchanged.

## What exists

- `JxlFrameHeader` parses the `Noise` and `Splines` flags (`HasNoise`, `HasSplines`).
  `Frames/JxlDecodeSupportPolicy.EnsureFrameSupported` and `Integration/JxlLayerCollector` throw
  `NotSupportedException("noise and splines are not rendered yet.")`.
- `Frames/JxlFrameDecoder.DecodeLfGlobal` reads patches and then the DC quantization; the spline and noise fields that
  sit between them in the LF global section are never reached, because the policy rejects the frame first.
- `Rendering/JxlFrameRenderer.Render` runs libjxl's `PreparePipeline` stages on whole-frame float planes: chroma
  upsampling, Gaborish, EPF, extra-channel upsampling, patches, upsampling, then the colour transform
  (`JxlColorOutput`). Two shortcuts bypass it partly: deferred YCbCr rows (`CanDeferChroma`, 0.8.6) and the integer
  samples of plain lossless frames (`JxlFrameDecoder.Finish`). `ExpandGray` turns one gray plane into three when a
  later stage needs colour planes.
- `Encoding/Lossy/JxlEpfSharpnessSearch` renders frames through `JxlFrameRenderer` inside the encoder.
- `JxlDecodeSession` decodes frames in codestream order (the preview frame is skipped by
  `JxlFrameSectionReader`); the frame reader and `JxlLayers` use the same session.
- Corpus: `lossy-noise.jxl` (`cjxl -d 1 --photon_noise_iso=3200`) is expected to throw in `JxlDecodeTests`,
  `JxlFrameReaderTests` and `JxlLayersTests`. The ignored conformance corpus has `noise`, `noise_5` (500×606) and
  `animation_spline`, `animation_spline_5` (320×320, 20 ms frames), with rms/peak tolerances 0.0001/0.004 and
  0.02/0.06 (the `_5` cases are the same images at a coarser level).

## What libjxl does (v0.12.0)

### Bitstream (dec_frame.cc `ProcessDCGlobal`)

Order in the LF global section: patches, **splines** (if the flag is set), **noise** (if set), DC quantization,
VarDCT global info, then the Modular global tree. After the VarDCT info (which gives the colour correlation map),
`Splines::InitializeDrawCache` runs with the frame's upsampled size and `cmap.base()` (`YtoXRatio(0)`,
`YtoBRatio(0)`; Modular frames keep the defaults 0 and 1).

- Noise (`dec_noise.cc DecodeNoise`): eight 10-bit values, `lut[i] = value / 1024`.
- Splines (`splines.cc Splines::Decode`): an entropy code with 6 contexts (`DecodeHistograms`), the number of
  splines − 1, the starting points (first absolute, then `UnpackSigned` deltas), a quantization adjustment, then per
  spline the number of control points, double-delta-coded control points, 3 × 32 colour DCT and 32 sigma DCT
  coefficients; `CheckANSFinalState` at the end. Limits (all Corrupt): splines and control points
  ≤ min(2^20, xsize × ysize / 2) in total; every coordinate and delta within ±2^23; delta-deltas within ±2^30; a DCT
  coefficient equal to `INT_MIN`; identical successive control points.
- `Dequantize`: control points from the double deltas; colours `q × weight × invQuant` (coefficient 0 × √½), then
  X += YtoX × Y and B += YtoB × Y; an area estimate from Manhattan distance, colour magnitude and sigma per spline,
  summed, must stay within `area_limit = min(1024 × pixels + 2^32, 2^42)` (Corrupt). Above
  `min(8 × pixels + 2^25, 2^30)` libjxl only warns, except in fuzzing builds, where it fails.

### Drawing (splines.cc, stage_splines.cc)

- Each spline: centripetal Catmull-Rom through the control points (16 points per segment, mirrored end points), then
  points spaced 1 pixel apart along the arc; at each point the colour and sigma are the continuous IDCT
  (`FastCosf`) of the DCTs at the point's position along the arc. Each point becomes a segment (centre, sigma,
  colour, intensity, maximum distance from `kDistanceExp = 5`, since `JXL_HIGH_PRECISION` is 1), with the rows it
  touches. Per row, the segments are found through a prefix-sum index (`segment_y_start`, `segment_indices`).
- `DrawSegment` adds `colour × σ/4 × intensity × (erf((d/2 + 0.3536)/σ) − erf((d/2 − 0.3536)/σ))²` (`FastErff`, d =
  distance to the centre) to the three colour channels for each pixel within the segment's span; the sum over segments is in segment order, so the result does
  not depend on how rows are split.
- Pipeline position (dec_cache.cc `PreparePipeline`): after patches, **before** upsampling; on the frame's own
  coordinates; colour channels only (in XYB for lossy frames, in the coded colour space for Modular frames).

### Noise (dec_noise.cc, stage_noise.cc, xorshift128plus-inl.h)

- Random input: three extra float planes of the upsampled frame size. For each group and each of the `upsampling²`
  sub-rectangles of `group_dim × group_dim` (clipped to the frame), one `Xorshift128Plus` (8 lanes, SplitMix64 seeding)
  is seeded with `(visible_frame_index, nonvisible_frame_index, x0, y0)` of the sub-rectangle and fills the three
  planes in turn, row by row: per row ⌈width/16⌉ batches of 8 × 64 bits, each 32-bit half → float `1 + bits/2^23`
  in [1, 2). The values depend only on the frame indices and the position, so groups can be filled in any order.
- `ConvolveNoiseStage`: on each random plane, `0.16 × (sum of the 5×5 neighbourhood without the centre) − 3.84 ×
  centre` (a Laplacian of the random field), with mirrored borders at the frame edges.
- `AddNoiseStage`: per pixel, `strength_r/g = LUT((Y ± X) / 2)` (linear interpolation over 8 points, scale 6, clamped
  to [0, 1]); `red = strength_r × (1/128 × rnd_r + 127/128 × rnd_c) × 0.22`, `green` likewise;
  `X += YtoX × (red + green) + red − green`, `Y += red + green`, `B += YtoB × (red + green)` with `cmap.base()` ratios.
  Skipped when every LUT entry is ≤ 1e-3.
- Position: after upsampling, before the frame is saved before the colour transform and before YCbCr/XYB → RGB. It
  also runs for DC frames (`dc_level ≠ 0`) and frames that are saved for reference.
- Frame indices (dec_frame.cc `InitFrame`): a frame that is displayed (`is_last` or duration > 0, regular or
  skip-progressive, not the preview) increments `visible_frame_index` and resets `nonvisible_frame_index` to 0; every
  other frame, the preview frame included, increments `nonvisible_frame_index`. Both start at 0 per decoder.
  So the grain is the same for the same frame position and differs between animation frames.

### Encoder (enc_frame.cc, enc_photon_noise.cc)

- `--photon_noise_iso=N` sets the noise flag on every non-DC frame and fills the LUT from `SimulatePhotonNoise(xsize,
  ysize, iso)`: a 36 × 24 mm sensor model (shot noise, read noise 3 e⁻, PRNU 0.5 %) in XYB, with `xsize`/`ysize` of
  the frame (after downsampling when the frame is upsampled, the cropped size for cropped frames); written as
  `lround(v × 1024)` in 10 bits. Nothing else in the bitstream changes (300×200: +12 bytes at d1).
- Modular frames also get the flag (`photon_noise_iso` bypasses "non-photon noise is not supported in Modular"), so
  `cjxl -d 0 -e 2…9 --photon_noise_iso` writes a "lossless" file whose djxl output differs from the source (measured:
  up to 2/255 on a 300×200 image); `-e 1` takes the fast lossless path and ignores the option. Lossy Modular
  (`--modular -d 1`) gets noise in XYB as VarDCT does.

## Design

### Decoding

1. **Reading.** `JxlFrameDecoder.DecodeLfGlobal` reads splines (new `Frames/JxlSplineReader`, using the existing
   `JxlEntropyCode` / ANS reader with 6 contexts) and noise (new `Model/JxlNoiseParameters`, 8 floats) between
   patches and the DC quantization, with every limit above as `ImageFormatException`. After `varDct.ReadGlobal` the
   splines are dequantized and turned into segments (`Rendering/JxlSplines.Prepare`) with the colour correlation
   base ratios (Modular: 0 and 1).
2. **Rendering.** `JxlFrameRenderer.Render` gets two stages in libjxl's order: `JxlSplines.Draw(color)` right after
   patches (before upsampling), and `JxlNoise.Apply(color, …)` right after upsampling, both before the colour
   transform and therefore before a frame is saved before the colour transform. Both work on whole-frame planes in
   row bands through `JxlParallel` (the per-pixel sums are in segment order and the random field does not depend on
   the split), so results are identical at 1 and N threads. Gray frames are expanded to three planes when either
   stage runs (`ExpandGray`), as libjxl always has three colour channels.
3. **Noise field.** `JxlNoise` fills three float planes of the upsampled frame size group by group (in parallel, one
   `JxlXorshift128Plus` per group sub-rectangle with libjxl's seeds), convolves them with mirrored borders and adds
   the noise. A LUT without any entry above 1e-3 skips everything, as libjxl does. The renderer receives the noise
   parameters, the frame indices and the colour correlation explicitly; `JxlEpfSharpnessSearch` (encoder) passes
   none, so the encoder's searches stay noise-free.
4. **Frame indices.** `JxlDecodeSession` counts `visible`/`nonvisible` exactly as `InitFrame` does, counting the
   preview frame as non-visible when the image has one, for every frame it reads, including frames it skips
   (`FirstFrameOnly`, frame reader) where the counters must still advance. `JxlLayers` and the frame reader use the
   same counters, so a frame's grain is the same through every API.
5. **Shortcuts.** `CanDeferChroma` and the integer lossless path are taken only when the frame has neither noise nor
   splines; a still image without them keeps today's path unchanged (golden gate).
6. **Precision.** Scalar ports with `MathF.FusedMultiplyAdd` wherever libjxl uses `MulAdd` (on x86 djxl runs AVX2,
   where MulAdd is FMA); `FastCosf` and `FastErff` ported bit for bit; the 32-term `ContinuousIDCT` accumulates in
   libjxl's AVX2 lane order (4 × 8 lanes, then the lane sum). Remaining differences to djxl (sqrt/log rounding) are
   far below 1/255; the conformance tolerances are checked in addition.
7. **Support policy.** `HasNoise`/`HasSplines` no longer throw in `EnsureFrameSupported` or `JxlLayerCollector`.
   `JxlLayers` renders each layer with its noise and splines like `djxl --no_coalescing`.
8. **SIMD.** Scalar first. A `JxlNoise.Simd.cs` / `JxlSplines.Simd.cs` only with a measured gain on the noise and
   spline corpus files (spline drawing is the likely candidate: one erf pair per pixel and segment).

### Limits and memory

- Noise: three float planes of the upsampled frame (12 bytes per pixel) plus the convolution output, charged to
  `JxlWorkBudget` before allocation and released with the frame's buffer scope (K = 16 samples per pixel; the corpus
  maximum rises from 9 to about 12–15).
- Splines: control points are bounded by libjxl's limits above (at most min(2^20, pixels / 2)); the segments (32
  bytes each, one per pixel of arc length) and the row index are charged to `JxlWorkBudget` before allocation, so a
  file whose splines would need more memory than `MaxPixels` allows fails with the usual working-memory error instead
  of allocating gigabytes. The drawing time guard is decision 2. Cancellation is checked per row band in both stages.

### Encoding

- `JxlEncoderOptions.PhotonNoiseIso` (`float?`, null = off; finite and > 0, else `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`):
  documented as `cjxl --photon_noise_iso`, the ISO of a full-frame 36 × 24 mm sensor whose grain is simulated by the
  decoder. When set, every lossy frame gets the noise flag and the LUT from a port of `SimulatePhotonNoise` (new
  `Encoding/JxlPhotonNoise`), written after the patches in the LF global section. The rest of the frame is encoded
  exactly as without the option (the encoder does not denoise), so file sizes grow by about 10 bytes per frame.
- Lossless: decision 1. Animations and cropped frames: decision 3.
- The option is part of `JxlFrameWriter` validation in `Open` like the other options.

### Not in scope

- Estimating noise from the image (`cjxl`'s automatic noise at distance ≥ 99 and `manual_noise`): no public option.
- Writing splines (libjxl's encoder only writes splines given explicitly; no detection exists).
- Removing noise or splines on decode (`render_noise = false`): no option; djxl always renders them.

## Deviations and results

- Noise is read in `JxlFrameDecoder` (the Model layer may not use the bit reader); a LUT without an entry above 1e-3 is
  dropped there, so such frames keep the integer and deferred-YCbCr shortcuts as libjxl's output is unchanged.
- No C++ harness for Xorshift128+, FastCosf and FastErff: the end-to-end comparisons below are bit exact for noise and
  splines, which covers the seeds, the lane order and the approximations; unit tests check determinism and accuracy.
- djxl decodes files with a negative first spline starting point as invalid too (the first point is unsigned).
- Corpus: 16 new files (cjxl: `noise-iso100`, `noise-iso51200`, `noise-alpha`, `noise-gray`, `noise-16bit`,
  `noise-resampling`, `noise-modular`, `anim-noise`; jxl_from_tree: `splines-xyb`, `splines-rgb`, `splines-up2`,
  `splines-noise`, `noise-up4`, `noise-groups`, `noise-layers`); `lossy-noise` is decoded. Every one against djxl:
  noise within 1/255 (most identical), splines identical, also through the frame reader and `JxlLayers`
  (`noise-layers` vs `djxl --no_coalescing`). Conformance `noise`/`noise_5` max 1/255, `animation_spline`/`_5`
  identical in all 60 frames. Ad-hoc checks: upsampling 2/4/8, group shift 0, RGB Modular, Gaborish + EPF with
  splines, all identical to djxl.
- Encoder: on flat 512×384 and 256×192 images our `PhotonNoiseIso` output decodes to exactly the pixels of cjxl's at
  ISO 100, 3200 and 51200 (same LUT); +10 bytes per frame. cjxl's own files shrink slightly with the option (its
  encoder search sees the noise flag); ours encode the frame unchanged.
- Checks: tests 1049/1049 (1050 with the SIMD test); golden vs 0.8.14: 168 lines, only the 16 noise/spline files (NotSupported → decoded),
  t1 == t16; fuzz seeds 30/31, 5 min each: decode 1.24 M, cancel-decode 352 k, frames 678 k, layers 1.21 M,
  encode-frames 25 k, no findings; encode 2.7 k with 3 timeouts that pass alone in about 6 s with or without noise
  (1826×1875 effort 7 encoded three times under 12 parallel workers); diff-decode 325 k: one ToSrgb mismatch of
  2/255 on a mutated gray animation that 0.8.14 decodes byte for byte the same (pre-existing), accept/lenient cases
  of the known container and metadata kinds. Pack + consumer JIT/AOT (now with photon noise) pass.
- Speed (scalar, 5 runs, median): IMG 3024×4032 d1: 284 ms without noise, 617 ms with ISO 6400 on one thread (djxl
  0.25/0.30 s); 4284×5712: 459/1109 ms (djxl 0.41/0.52 s); all threads 260/372 and 338/375 ms (djxl 0.06–0.11 s).
  Conformance `noise` 77/30 ms (1/all threads), `animation_spline` 60 frames 158/265 ms. Scalar noise was about 6×
  slower than libjxl's vector code.
- SIMD (user, 2026-11-02): `JxlNoise.Simd.cs` (5×5 convolution and noise addition 8 pixels at a time, the LUT through
  `PermuteVar8x32`, mantissa floats) and `JxlXorshift128Plus.Simd.cs` (4 lanes per vector), AVX2+FMA with the scalar
  order of operations, bit-identical to the frozen scalar copy (`ScalarBaseline/JxlNoiseScalar.cs`,
  `JxlSimdTests.Noise_ExactAcrossWidthsAndSpecialValues`, also in the SSE4.1 and scalar modes of
  `scripts/verify-simd.sh`) and to djxl as before. One thread: the noise stage costs 65 ms on 12 MP (was 333 ms) and
  134 ms on 24 MP (was 650 ms); djxl's noise costs about 50 and 110 ms. 3024×4032 ISO 6400 decodes in 351 ms
  (no noise 286 ms), 4284×5712 in 585 ms (451 ms). Splines stay scalar (cheap in every measured file).

## Open decisions (user)

None open.

Decided (user, 2026-11-02):

1. (a) `PhotonNoiseIso` with lossless output throws `ArgumentException`; lossless output stays exact.
2. (a) libjxl's production spline limits (Corrupt above the hard area limit), segment memory charged to the working
   budget, cancellation per row band; no fuzzing-build limit.
3. (a) The noise LUT of every animation frame uses the full canvas size (divided by the upsampling factor).

Options as proposed:

1. **`PhotonNoiseIso` with lossless output.** cjxl writes the noise flag into lossless Modular frames at effort ≥ 2
   (output no longer exact) and ignores it at effort 1.
   - (a, recommended) Reject the combination: `Lossless`/distance 0 with `PhotonNoiseIso` throws
     `ArgumentException` when encoding starts (or in the frame writer's `Open`). Keeps the AGENTS.md rule that
     lossless output decodes exactly, and avoids cjxl's effort-dependent behaviour.
   - (b) Like cjxl: lossless frames at effort ≥ 2 get the noise flag (documented as no longer exact), effort 1 ignores
     the option.
   - (c) Silently ignore the option for lossless output.
2. **Drawing time of splines.** libjxl's hard area limit allows up to ~1024 × pixels of estimated spline area (a few
   kilobytes of input can then cost minutes of drawing); its fuzzing builds fail above min(8 × pixels + 2^25, 2^30).
   - (a, recommended) libjxl's production rules (Corrupt above the hard limit), plus the memory charge above and
     cancellation per row band; long draws stay possible but cancellable, and every file djxl decodes still decodes.
   - (b) Also fail above the fuzzing-build limit, with the working-memory error, protecting servers from slow inputs
     at the price of rejecting rare files djxl accepts.
3. **Noise LUT for animation frames.** cjxl computes the LUT from each frame's own size; our animation encoder crops
   frames to the changed rectangle, so cropped frames would get a different (weaker) grain than full frames.
   - (a, recommended) Use the full canvas size (divided by the upsampling factor, as cjxl does for full frames) for
     every frame: the same grain on every frame, identical to cjxl for stills and uncropped frames.
   - (b) Like cjxl: each frame's own (cropped) size.
